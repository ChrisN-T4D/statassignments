Solving for a Variable

Often a formula gives you everything except one quantity, and you need to find it. To solve for a variable, undo the operations around it, in reverse order, doing the same thing to both sides.

Example 1: a simple equation

Solve 3x − 6 = 12.

  1. Add 6 to both sides: 3x = 18.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 6.
  3. Check: 3(6) − 6 = 12. ✓

Example 2: a formula in words

An average is total ÷ count. A class of 20 students has an average of 15. What is the total? Multiply both sides by the count:

total = count × average = 20 × 15 = 300.

Example 3: a formula with two steps

Solve y = 3x + 5 for x when y = 26.

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides: 21 = 3x.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 7.
  3. Check: 3(7) + 5 = 26. ✓

The skill is the same every time: undo, in reverse order, on both sides. You will use it later to rearrange formulas to find a different unknown.

Key Takeaways

  • Undo operations in reverse order and do the same thing to both sides.
  • Always check your answer by putting it back into the original equation.
  • Any formula can be rearranged to find a different unknown.
