Reading Graphs

Graphs show data as shapes. Before you read the shapes, read the frame.

Check these first

  1. Title and labels. What is on the horizontal (x) axis and the vertical (y) axis? Are there units?
  2. Scale. Where does the axis start? Does it start at 0? A bar chart axis that starts at 50 makes small differences look huge.
  3. What does one mark represent? One bar, one dot, or one line segment may each mean something different.

Three graphs you will see

  • Bar chart: one bar per category (for example, major). Bars are separated, and the height shows a count or a mean.
  • Histogram: bars touch because the x-axis is a number line cut into intervals. The height shows how many scores fall in each interval, so it shows the shape of the data.
  • Scatterplot: each dot is one person measured twice. A dot high and to the right scored high on both variables (for example, many hours studied and a high exam score).

Reading a point

To read a point, go straight down to the x-axis for its horizontal value, then straight across to the y-axis for its vertical value. Right means more of x, and up means more of y.

Key Takeaways

  • Read the axis labels and where the scale starts before you read the shapes.
  • Bar charts show categories; histograms show the shape of a number line.
  • In a scatterplot, each dot is one person measured on two variables.
