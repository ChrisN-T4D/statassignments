Algebra Symbols in Formulas

Statistics formulas are written in the language of algebra. The symbols on this page are not statistics symbols. They are ordinary algebra and pre-calculus notation that statistics borrows. If you have seen them in a college algebra class, this page is a quick reminder. If not, you can learn all of them here.

Same symbol, different job

  • A superscript (x2) is a power. A subscript (x2) is only a label. They are not interchangeable.
  • Capital Σ means “add.” Lowercase σ will mean something different later in the course. They look alike but are different symbols.
  • A minus sign in front of a number and a subtraction between two numbers use the same mark. Parentheses tell you which you have.

Putting them together

The expression √[ Σ(x − 4)2 / 3 ] uses almost every symbol above. Read it from the inside out: subtract 4 from each x (parentheses), square each result (exponent), add them up (Σ), divide by 3 (fraction bar), and take the square root (radical).

Most of these symbols have their own page in this module with practice. The letters that statistics adds on top will be introduced when you first need them.

Key Takeaways

  • These are algebra symbols that statistics borrows. They are not specific to statistics.
  • Read an expression from the inside out: parentheses, then powers and roots, then sums, then division.
  • A superscript is a power; a subscript is only a label.
  • Capital Σ means add; lowercase σ is a different symbol you will meet later.
