Order of Operations

Statistics formulas pack several steps into one line. The order of operations tells you which step to do first. Do the steps in this order:

  1. Parentheses (brackets) first, working from the innermost pair outward
  2. Exponents (squares) and roots
  3. Multiplication and Division, left to right
  4. Addition and Subtraction, left to right

Worked examples

ExpressionStepsAnswer
4 + 3 × 2222 = 4; 3 × 4 = 12; 4 + 1216
(4 + 3) × 22(4 + 3) = 7; 22 = 4; 7 × 428
12 ÷ (2 + 4)(2 + 4) = 6; 12 ÷ 62
12 ÷ 2 + 412 ÷ 2 = 6; 6 + 410

Notice that the same numbers give 16 or 28 depending on the brackets. Brackets are never decoration.

A longer example

Later formulas pack many steps into one line. Take √[ ( (2 − 4)2 + (6 − 4)2 ) ÷ 2 ]. Work from the inside out:

  1. Subtract inside the innermost brackets: (2 − 4) = −2 and (6 − 4) = 2.
  2. Square each: (−2)2 = 4 and 22 = 4.
  3. Add them: 4 + 4 = 8.
  4. Divide by 2: 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
  5. Take the square root: √4 = 2.

Squaring before subtracting would give a different, wrong answer.

Calculator caution

Type brackets exactly as written. To find 12 ÷ (2 + 4), enter the brackets, or you will get 10 instead of 2. Also, (−3)2 = 9 but −32 = −9. Put the negative number in brackets when you square it.

Key Takeaways

  • Brackets first, then exponents and roots, then multiply and divide, then add and subtract.
  • In a long formula, work from the innermost brackets outward.
  • Type brackets on your calculator exactly as the formula shows them.
